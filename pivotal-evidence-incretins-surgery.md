# Pivotal evidence module 2: GLP-1/GIP-based therapies and metabolic surgery

Updated: November 10, 2026  
Scope: randomized trials and systematic reviews in adults with type 2 diabetes and overweight or obesity, with selected withdrawal and outcomes trials in adjacent populations when they answer a distinct question. This is an evidence extraction, not individualized treatment advice.

## Bottom line

- In adults with type 2 diabetes and overweight or obesity, semaglutide 2.4 mg and tirzepatide 10–15 mg produce substantial average weight loss and improve glycemia over roughly 68–72 weeks. Average weight loss in the pivotal trials was larger with tirzepatide, but the two obesity-dose programs were not directly randomized against each other in this population.
- A direct diabetes trial found greater A1C and weight reductions with tirzepatide than semaglutide, but it compared tirzepatide 5–15 mg with semaglutide 1.0 mg—not semaglutide 2.4 mg—and lasted 40 weeks.
- Medication withdrawal studies in people without diabetes show substantial weight regain after semaglutide or tirzepatide is stopped. They support viewing obesity pharmacotherapy as potentially long-term treatment, not a short course with a durable average effect after cessation.
- Metabolic surgery generally produces larger and more durable weight loss and more frequent diabetes remission than medical treatment in randomized trials, but the trials are small, open label, and imprecise for mortality, uncommon harms, and differences among procedures.
- Weight loss, A1C reduction, remission, cardiovascular events, kidney failure, quality of life, treatment burden, and harms are different outcomes. They should not be collapsed into a single ranking.

## What happens after treatment stops

| Study | Population and design | Withdrawal result | Interpretation boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| [STEP 1 extension](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35441470/) (2022) | Exploratory off-treatment follow-up of 327 participants from a semaglutide trial; participants had overweight or obesity but not diabetes | After mean loss of 17.3% at week 68 with semaglutide 2.4 mg, participants regained 11.6 percentage points by week 120—about two-thirds of the prior loss. Many cardiometabolic measures moved back toward baseline. | Selected extension subset, no diabetes, and no continued randomized treatment after week 68. It demonstrates substantial average regain after withdrawal but does not predict every individual's trajectory or the exact effect in type 2 diabetes. |
| [SURMOUNT-4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38078870/) (2024) | Randomized withdrawal trial after a 36-week open-label tirzepatide lead-in; 670 participants with overweight or obesity but not diabetes were randomized | From week 36 to 88, continued tirzepatide produced an additional 5.5% loss, while switching to placebo produced 14.0% regain. At least 80% of lead-in weight loss was maintained by 89.5% versus 16.6%. | The randomized phase directly estimates continued treatment versus withdrawal among people who tolerated and completed the lead-in. It does not represent treatment-naive randomization and excluded diabetes. |

These withdrawal data do not mean that every person must remain on the same drug indefinitely. They do show that durable benefit after stopping cannot be assumed and that maintenance, adverse effects, cost, access, and preferences belong in the treatment question from the start.

## Clinical-outcome boundary

The weight-management trials above mainly establish weight and glycemic effects. They should not be cited as if those endpoints automatically prove fewer heart attacks, kidney failures, or deaths.

There is separate outcomes evidence, which will be extracted fully in module 3:

- In [SELECT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37952131/), semaglutide 2.4 mg reduced cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke from 8.0% to 6.5% (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.72–0.90) over a mean 39.8 months in 17,604 adults with established cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity **without diabetes**. Permanent treatment discontinuation due to adverse events was 16.6% versus 8.2%.
- A [2024 meta-analysis of large GLP-1 receptor agonist outcomes trials](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39608381/) found, among 67,769 participants with type 2 diabetes, reductions in major cardiovascular events (HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.81–0.93), a composite kidney outcome (HR 0.82, 0.73–0.93), kidney failure (HR 0.84, 0.72–0.99), and all-cause death (HR 0.88, 0.83–0.93). Discontinuation for adverse events was more frequent (RR 1.51, 1.18–1.94). Class-level pooling does not establish identical effects for every molecule, dose, or patient group.

## Safety and treatment-burden boundary

For incretin-based drugs, common trial burdens include gastrointestinal symptoms, dose escalation, injections for the products studied here, treatment discontinuation, cost, coverage changes, shortages, and weight recurrence after stopping. Rare adverse events require larger safety datasets than the weight trials alone.

Metabolic surgery has perioperative risks and procedure-specific long-term burdens. The [ADA Standards of Care 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article-pdf/49/Supplement_1/S166/848839/dc26s008.pdf) emphasize experienced high-volume centers, psychological and social assessment, long-term medical and behavioral support, routine nutritional and micronutrient monitoring, and surveillance for weight recurrence and post-surgical hypoglycemia. Longer-term risks include nutritional deficiencies, anemia, osteoporosis, dumping syndrome, and hypoglycemia. These burdens are part of the comparison, not footnotes to a remission percentage.

## Evidence judgment

1. **Weight and glycemia:** high confidence that semaglutide and tirzepatide improve both over approximately one year in the studied populations; effect magnitude depends on drug, dose, adherence, estimand, background therapy, and population.
2. **Head-to-head comparison:** direct evidence supports tirzepatide over semaglutide 1.0 mg for 40-week A1C and weight outcomes, but it does not directly compare the obesity doses used in STEP 2 and SURMOUNT-2.
3. **Durability:** strong evidence that continued therapy maintains more weight loss than withdrawal in selected completers; exact long-term outcomes in type 2 diabetes remain less certain.
4. **Surgery:** randomized evidence supports larger long-term weight loss and more remission than medical therapy, but long-term certainty for mortality, uncommon harms, and procedure rankings is limited by small trials.
5. **Choice of strategy:** no single trial resolves the patient-level tradeoff among efficacy, clinical outcomes, adverse effects, reversibility, monitoring, cost, access, treatment burden, and personal preference.
