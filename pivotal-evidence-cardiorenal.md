# Pivotal evidence module 3: cardiovascular and kidney outcomes

Updated: November 10, 2026  
Scope: large randomized cardiovascular and kidney outcome trials of SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1–based therapies, emphasizing adults with type 2 diabetes and including trials without diabetes when they establish a distinct cardiorenal effect. This is an evidence extraction, not individualized treatment advice.

## Bottom line

- SGLT2 inhibitors have strong randomized evidence for reducing heart-failure hospitalization and slowing chronic kidney disease progression. Dedicated kidney trials show benefit in people with and without type 2 diabetes, so the effect is not adequately described as glucose lowering alone.
- Several GLP-1 receptor agonists reduce major adverse cardiovascular events in type 2 diabetes. FLOW additionally provides dedicated kidney-outcome evidence for semaglutide in type 2 diabetes with chronic kidney disease.
- Benefits are not interchangeable across all drugs. Empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin, liraglutide, dulaglutide, and semaglutide have different trial populations, comparators, doses, endpoints, and follow-up.
- Relative hazard ratios do not reveal the likely personal benefit by themselves. Absolute benefit is generally larger when baseline cardiovascular, heart-failure, or kidney risk is higher.
- A composite outcome is only as interpretable as its components. Kidney composites, for example, differ in required eGFR decline, kidney-failure thresholds, and whether cardiovascular death is included.

## SGLT2 inhibitors: dedicated kidney trials

| Study | Population and design | Follow-up | Primary result | Applicability and limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [CREDENCE](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30990260/) (2019) | 4,401 adults with type 2 diabetes, eGFR 30 to under 90, and albuminuria above 300 to 5,000 mg/g while receiving renin–angiotensin system blockade; canagliflozin 100 mg versus placebo | Median 2.62 years; stopped early for efficacy | End-stage kidney disease, doubling of serum creatinine, or kidney/cardiovascular death occurred at 43.2 versus 61.2 events per 1,000 patient-years (HR 0.70, 0.59–0.82). | Strong evidence in albuminuric diabetic kidney disease on background blockade. Early stopping and a high-risk, albuminuric population limit extrapolation to people with normal kidney function or little albuminuria. Industry funded. |
| [DAPA-CKD](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32970396/) (2020) | 4,304 adults with eGFR 25–75 and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio 200–5,000 mg/g, with or without type 2 diabetes; dapagliflozin 10 mg versus placebo | Median 2.4 years; stopped early for efficacy | Sustained at least 50% eGFR decline, end-stage kidney disease, or kidney/cardiovascular death occurred in 9.2% versus 14.5% (HR 0.61, 0.51–0.72); the reported number needed to treat was 19 over the trial period. Cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalization had HR 0.71, and all-cause death was 4.7% versus 6.8% (HR 0.69). | Similar relative effects with and without diabetes support a nonglycemic component. The trial required substantial albuminuria and does not answer prevention in low-risk kidneys. Industry funded. |
| [EMPA-KIDNEY](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36331190/) (2023) | 6,609 adults with chronic kidney disease, with or without diabetes; empagliflozin 10 mg versus placebo. Eligibility included eGFR 20 to under 45 regardless of albuminuria, or eGFR 45 to under 90 with albumin-to-creatinine ratio at least 200 mg/g | Median 2.0 years; stopped early for efficacy | Kidney-disease progression or cardiovascular death occurred in 13.1% versus 16.9% (HR 0.72, 0.64–0.82). | Broader kidney population than CREDENCE and DAPA-CKD, including many without diabetes and some with low albuminuria. Short follow-up and relatively few cardiovascular events limited precision for cardiovascular death, heart-failure hospitalization, and mortality separately. Industry and other funding. |

These kidney trials should not be compared by ranking their hazard ratios. Their entry criteria, baseline risks, follow-up, and composite definitions differ substantially.

## Adjacent evidence and class-level synthesis

- [SELECT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37952131/) showed that semaglutide 2.4 mg reduced major cardiovascular events from 8.0% to 6.5% (HR 0.80, 0.72–0.90) in 17,604 adults with established cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity **without diabetes**. This strengthens the case that cardiovascular benefit is not solely mediated by glucose lowering, but it does not directly answer treatment in lower-risk people or people with diabetes.
- A [2025 GLP-1 receptor agonist meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39608381/) included 11 trials and 85,373 participants. Among 67,769 participants with type 2 diabetes, pooled HRs were 0.87 for MACE, 0.82 for a clinical kidney composite, 0.84 for kidney failure, and 0.88 for all-cause death. Serious adverse events did not differ overall, but discontinuation for adverse events was more frequent (RR 1.51). Heterogeneity in molecules, endpoints, and populations means these pooled estimates are context, not a promise for every product.
- The [ADA Standards of Care 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S183/163934/9-Pharmacologic-Approaches-to-Glycemic-Treatment) recommend an SGLT2 inhibitor and/or GLP-1 receptor agonist with demonstrated benefit for people with type 2 diabetes and established or high risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or chronic kidney disease, independently of the need for additional glucose lowering or metformin use. This recommendation is comorbidity-driven and does not imply that an otherwise low-risk person should self-start therapy.

## How to read the numbers

1. **Hazard ratio is not absolute risk reduction.** A hazard ratio of 0.75 can correspond to a large or small absolute benefit depending on untreated risk and follow-up.
2. **Trial duration matters.** A crude difference in cumulative event percentages applies only approximately to the reported follow-up and is not a lifetime estimate.
3. **Composite endpoints can hide divergent components.** DECLARE reduced cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalization through heart-failure hospitalization, not cardiovascular death. EMPA-REG reduced cardiovascular death but not myocardial infarction or stroke individually.
4. **Early stopping matters.** CREDENCE, DAPA-CKD, EMPA-KIDNEY, and FLOW stopped early after efficacy boundaries were crossed; this is ethically appropriate but can reduce information about long-term durability and uncommon harms.
5. **Background care matters.** Benefits were added to contemporary risk-factor management, not substituted for blood-pressure control, statins when indicated, smoking cessation, renin–angiotensin system blockade when appropriate, or diabetes self-management.
6. **Cross-trial rankings are unreliable.** Different drugs were not randomized against each other across these trials, with the exception of SURPASS-CVOT's active comparison of tirzepatide and dulaglutide.

## Safety and treatment-burden boundary

SGLT2 inhibitors can cause genital infections and volume depletion and rarely diabetic ketoacidosis, which may occur without extreme hyperglycemia. Risk rises in settings such as acute illness, prolonged fasting, major surgery, marked insulin deficiency, or inappropriate insulin reduction. Kidney function and volume status influence initiation and monitoring. The early eGFR dip after starting treatment can be hemodynamic rather than progressive injury, but it still requires clinical interpretation.

GLP-1–based therapies commonly cause nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, or treatment discontinuation, especially during dose escalation. Individual contraindications, kidney function, concurrent glucose-lowering therapy, planned procedures, pregnancy considerations, access, and product labeling matter. Neither class should be selected from a trial table without a clinician reviewing the person's diagnoses, medicines, kidney function, and risks.

## Evidence judgment

1. **Heart failure:** high confidence that SGLT2 inhibitors with demonstrated benefit reduce heart-failure hospitalization across relevant high-risk populations; absolute benefit depends on baseline risk.
2. **Chronic kidney disease:** high confidence that studied SGLT2 inhibitors slow clinically important progression in CKD, including in people without diabetes. High confidence also supports semaglutide 1.0 mg in the FLOW population with type 2 diabetes and CKD.
3. **Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease:** high confidence that selected GLP-1 receptor agonists reduce MACE in type 2 diabetes; SGLT2 MACE results are more population- and molecule-dependent even when heart-failure and kidney benefits are present.
4. **Mortality:** some individual high-risk trials show mortality benefit, but it should not be assumed uniformly across the classes or transported to low-risk populations.
5. **Personal application:** clinical benefit cannot be inferred from short-term CGM changes. A meal-level glucose curve is a useful behavioral measurement but is not a surrogate for the cardiovascular and kidney outcomes above.
