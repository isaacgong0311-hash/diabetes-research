# Selected randomized evidence: diabetes prevention and treatment

Prepared October 31, 2026. This is a **purposeful starter set**, not a systematic review, meta-analysis, or complete risk-of-bias assessment. Findings are extracted from the linked primary publications and abstracts; full protocol, missing-data, and detailed adverse-event appraisal remain for a formal review. The trials differ in population, intervention, comparator, outcome, and follow-up. Their numbers should not be ranked as if they were head-to-head comparisons.

## 1. Preventing type 2 diabetes: Diabetes Prevention Program

- **Population and design:** 3,234 adults without diabetes but with elevated fasting and post-load glucose; randomized to placebo, metformin, or an intensive lifestyle program.
- **Follow-up and outcome:** Mean 2.8 years; incident diabetes. Rates were 11.0, 7.8, and 4.8 cases per 100 person-years in placebo, metformin, and lifestyle groups, respectively. Relative to placebo, reported incidence reductions were 31% for metformin and 58% for lifestyle.
- **Interpretation:** These are incidence rates, not percentages of participants developing diabetes. The lifestyle intervention was a structured program with specific activity and weight goals, not a simple advice handout.
- **Applicability/limitations:** Participants were selected for high diabetes risk. The results should not be applied as a universal effect size for every person with prediabetes. Long-term follow-up involved changes in intervention exposure.
- **Source:** [Knowler et al., 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11832527/); [10-year follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19878986/).

## 2. Remission with a structured weight-management program: DiRECT

- **Population and design:** Primary-care cluster-randomized trial in adults aged 20–65 with type 2 diabetes diagnosed within six years, BMI 27–45, and no insulin treatment. The intervention used supervised formula-diet replacement, food reintroduction, and ongoing support; the control received usual care.
- **Outcome:** At 12 months, 68 of 149 participants (46%) in the intervention group and 6 of 149 (4%) in the control group met the trial's remission definition: A1C below 6.5% after at least two months without glucose-lowering medication.
- **Harms:** Serious adverse events occurred in seven intervention participants and two controls; two events in one intervention participant were considered potentially related to the intervention.
- **Applicability/limitations:** Open-label, cluster-randomized, selected early-disease population; remission is not synonymous with cure and needs ongoing follow-up. The five-year extension had a selected follow-up sample and different denominators; its results should not be compared directly with the original 12-month randomized percentages.
- **Funding/source:** Diabetes UK. [Lean et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29221645/); [five-year extension](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-8587(23)00385-6).

## 3. Lifestyle and cardiovascular events: Look AHEAD

- **Population and design:** 5,145 adults with type 2 diabetes and overweight or obesity randomized to intensive lifestyle intervention or diabetes support and education.
- **Follow-up and outcome:** At median 9.6 years, the primary cardiovascular composite occurred at 1.83 versus 1.92 events per 100 person-years; hazard ratio 0.95 (95% CI 0.83–1.09). The trial stopped after a futility analysis. Weight loss and some risk factors improved more in the intervention group.
- **Interpretation:** This trial did not demonstrate a reduction in the specified cardiovascular composite. It does not show that lifestyle change has no other benefits, or that all approaches to weight loss have the same cardiovascular effects.
- **Applicability/limitations:** Comparator received support and education, and the trial's intervention, baseline risk, co-interventions, and achieved weight loss matter. A nonsignificant result does not prove the true effect is exactly zero.
- **Funding/source:** NIH and others. [Look AHEAD Research Group, 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23796131/).

## 4. Medication comparisons on glycemic durability: GRADE

- **Population and design:** 5,047 metformin-treated adults with type 2 diabetes of less than 10 years' duration, randomized to add glargine, glimepiride, liraglutide, or sitagliptin; mean follow-up five years.
- **Primary outcome:** Confirmed A1C at or above 7.0%. Rates per 100 participant-years were 26.5, 30.4, 26.1, and 38.1, respectively. Glargine and liraglutide maintained the glycemic target somewhat longer than the other comparators.
- **Harms:** In the primary report, severe hypoglycemia occurred in 2.2% with glimepiride, 1.3% with glargine, 1.0% with liraglutide, and 0.7% with sitagliptin. Liraglutide had more gastrointestinal adverse effects and greater weight loss.
- **Applicability/limitations:** The main endpoint is glycemic deterioration, not mortality or a kidney outcome. This trial did not compare an SGLT2 inhibitor or newer high-potency GLP-1/GIP agents. Rates per participant-year are not cumulative percentages.
- **Funding/source:** NIDDK and others. [GRADE Study Research Group, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36129996/).

## 5. Kidney outcomes in high-risk type 2 diabetes: FLOW

- **Population and design:** 3,533 adults with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease at high risk, randomized to once-weekly semaglutide 1 mg or placebo.
- **Follow-up and outcome:** At median 3.4 years, the primary kidney/cardiovascular-death composite occurred in 331/1,767 versus 410/1,766 participants (approximately 18.7% vs 23.2%; hazard ratio 0.76, 95% CI 0.66–0.88). The crude difference in proportions is approximately 4.5 percentage points over this trial's follow-up; it is not interchangeable with the time-to-event hazard ratio.
- **Harms:** Serious adverse events were reported in 49.6% versus 53.8%. Gastrointestinal adverse events can affect tolerability; consult the full publication and current drug labeling for a complete adverse-event and contraindication assessment.
- **Applicability/limitations:** Selected chronic kidney disease population, prespecified early stopping, and relatively limited use of some newer background kidney-protective therapies limit direct extrapolation to all patients or treatment combinations. Event components differ in clinical importance.
- **Funding/source:** Novo Nordisk. [Perkovic et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38785209/); [primary full-text PDF](https://mediacenteratypon.nejmgroup-production.org/NEJMoa2403347.pdf).

## 6. Kidney outcomes with and without diabetes: EMPA-KIDNEY

- **Population and design:** 6,609 adults with chronic kidney disease, with or without diabetes, randomized to empagliflozin or placebo.
- **Follow-up and outcome:** During median two-year follow-up, kidney disease progression or cardiovascular death occurred in 432/3,304 (13.1%) versus 558/3,305 (16.9%); hazard ratio 0.72 (95% CI 0.64–0.82). The difference in observed group proportions was 3.8 percentage points.
- **Harms and limitations:** The primary abstract reports similar rates of serious adverse events overall; this does not establish that individual adverse effects are identical. The trial was not limited to diabetes, and the composite endpoint and eligibility criteria should be considered before extrapolating to an individual.
- **Funding/source:** Boehringer Ingelheim and others. [EMPA-KIDNEY Collaborative Group, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36331190/).

## 7. Continuous glucose monitoring with basal insulin: MOBILE

- **Population and design:** 175 adults with type 2 diabetes treated with basal insulin without mealtime insulin were randomized to continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) or a blood glucose meter.
- **Follow-up and outcome:** At eight months, A1C fell to 8.0% in the CGM group and 8.4% in the meter group; the adjusted difference was −0.4 percentage points (95% CI −0.8 to −0.1). Mean time in the specified glucose range was 59% versus 43%.
- **Harms and limitations:** Severe hypoglycemia was reported in one person in each group. The study was relatively small, lasted eight months, and used A1C as its primary endpoint; it does not establish long-term complication or mortality effects. Results should not automatically be generalized to all people not using insulin.
- **Source:** [Martens et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34077499/); [trial registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03566693).

## 8. Metabolic surgery versus medical/lifestyle care: ARMMS-T2D

- **Population and design:** Pooled long-term follow-up of four randomized trials; 262 of 305 eligible participants entered the follow-up study. Participants had originally been assigned to metabolic surgery or medical/lifestyle care.
- **Outcome:** At seven years, the between-group difference in A1C change was −1.4 percentage points (95% CI −1.8 to −1.0), and remission was 18.2% versus 6.2%. Follow-up extended to 12 years.
- **Harms:** Anemia, fractures, and gastrointestinal adverse events were more common following surgery.
- **Applicability/limitations:** This is pooled observational long-term follow-up of originally randomized groups, with 25% of the initially medical/lifestyle group crossing over to surgery; attrition, several procedures, and changing treatment options complicate interpretation. It does not establish which specific procedure is best for an individual.
- **Source:** [Courcoulas et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38411644/).

## Evidence interpretation checklist

Before using a result, verify the original trial's population and baseline risk, intervention and comparator, outcome definitions, follow-up, losses and crossover, event counts, absolute and relative effects, harms, funding, and applicability. Primary and secondary outcomes should not be conflated. Trial results should be integrated with current guidelines and individualized clinical assessment; this register is research material, not a prescription.
