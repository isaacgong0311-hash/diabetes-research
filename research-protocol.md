# Adult Type 2 Diabetes Research Protocol

Version: 0.1  
Prepared: October 31, 2026  
Review type: rapid scoping review

## Working question

Among nonpregnant adults with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes, which screening, lifestyle, pharmacologic, technology, and complication-monitoring strategies are supported by current evidence for improving glycemic, cardiovascular, kidney, weight, safety, and patient-centered outcomes?

## Purpose and audience

This project will produce a readable, evidence-based overview for an informed general audience. It is not a clinical decision aid for a particular patient and does not replace medical care.

## Scope

Included:

- Adults with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes
- Screening and diagnostic thresholds
- Prevention or delay of type 2 diabetes
- Glycemic and weight management
- Cardiovascular and kidney risk reduction
- Diabetes technology
- Screening for common complications
- Access, cost, treatment burden, and disparities
- U.S. population data and internationally relevant evidence where useful

Excluded from the initial review:

- Type 1 diabetes as the primary population
- Gestational diabetes and pregnancy management
- Pediatric diabetes
- Inpatient and perioperative care
- Individualized medication or insulin dosing
- Supplements or alternative treatments without credible comparative evidence

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Current clinical standards and government surveillance reports
2. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses of randomized trials
3. Large cardiovascular, kidney, and weight-outcome randomized trials
4. High-quality prospective observational studies
5. Expert consensus when comparative outcome evidence is unavailable

Guideline recommendations will not be treated as if they were all equally supported. The final synthesis will distinguish randomized outcome evidence, surrogate-outcome evidence, observational evidence, and expert opinion.

## Search plan

Core sources:

- PubMed/MEDLINE
- Cochrane Library
- American Diabetes Association (ADA) Standards of Care
- Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)
- National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK)
- ClinicalTrials.gov for unresolved questions and active studies

Initial concepts:

`type 2 diabetes` AND (`screening` OR `prevention` OR `lifestyle` OR `weight loss` OR `metformin` OR `GLP-1 receptor agonist` OR `tirzepatide` OR `SGLT2 inhibitor` OR `insulin` OR `continuous glucose monitoring` OR `cardiovascular outcomes` OR `kidney outcomes` OR `retinopathy` OR `neuropathy`)

## Eligibility criteria

- Publication date: prioritize 2021–2026; include older landmark trials where necessary
- Language: English for the rapid review
- Population: nonpregnant adults with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes
- Study types: guidelines with transparent methods, systematic reviews, randomized trials, and strong observational studies
- Outcomes: mortality, cardiovascular events, heart-failure hospitalization, kidney failure or CKD progression, diabetes incidence, remission, A1C, hypoglycemia, weight, quality of life, treatment burden, adverse events, and access

## Data extraction fields

- Citation and year
- Study design and setting
- Population and sample size
- Intervention and comparator
- Follow-up
- Primary and patient-important outcomes
- Absolute and relative effects, when available
- Adverse events
- Applicability
- Risk of bias and major limitations
- Funding and relevant conflicts of interest

## Planned outputs

1. Current-state research brief
2. Structured source log
3. Evidence table of pivotal trials and systematic reviews
4. Final report with clearly labeled findings, uncertainties, and research gaps

## Quality controls

- Verify numerical claims against original sources.
- Prefer absolute risks over relative risks when both are available.
- Do not infer class effects when evidence exists only for a particular drug or trial population.
- Separate glucose lowering from cardiovascular, kidney, and weight outcomes.
- Record the literature-search date and flag evidence likely to change quickly.
- Use person-first, non-stigmatizing language.
